D · Academic explanation

Key concepts

Supervised learning

The model learns a mapping from inputs to a known output using labelled examples, here the rows of the CSV. It is then used to predict the output for new inputs.

Classification vs regression

Classification predicts a category (rain / no rain). Regression predicts a continuous number (Cloud_Cover).

Dependent & independent variables

The dependent variable (target, Y) is what we predict. The independent variables (features, X) are the inputs. For the regression, Y = Cloud_Cover and X = Temperature, Humidity, Wind_Speed, Pressure.

Training & testing data

80% of the rows train the models. The other 20% are locked away and used once at the end to measure performance on data the models have never seen. Scalers are fitted on training data only, to avoid data leakage.

Cross-validation

The training data is split into 5 folds. Each fold takes one turn as a mini validation set. This tunes hyper-parameters and chooses between models without touching the test set.

Overfitting

A model overfits when it memorises noise in the training data: training scores are much better than test scores. Compare the training and test rows in the tables above.

Evaluation metrics

Precision: of the predicted rain cases, how many were rain. Recall: of the actual rain cases, how many were caught. F1: their harmonic mean. ROC-AUC: how well the probabilities rank rain above no-rain across all thresholds. R²: share of variance explained. MAE / RMSE: average error size; RMSE penalises large errors more.

Why compare two models?

A simple, interpretable baseline (linear) shows whether a more flexible model (Random Forest) is actually worth its extra complexity. Without a baseline, a score has nothing to be compared against.

Limitations

  • Unknown provenance and units. The CSV came without documentation, so units are not shown and the data may not represent real weather.
  • Likely synthetic labels. Rain follows simple thresholds (see the audit), so high classification scores will not transfer to real-world forecasting.
  • No signal for cloud cover. The four independent variables explain almost none of Cloud_Cover's variation, so regression predictions are close to the average value.
  • Probabilities are model estimates. Class weighting raises rain probabilities above the {{ metrics.dataset.target_distribution['rain'].percent }}% base rate. They are useful for ranking, not as calibrated real-world chances (see the Brier scores).
  • One split, one dataset. Results may change with a different random split or new data. No time or location information is available.
  • Extrapolation. Inputs outside the training ranges are accepted, with a warning, but predictions there are unreliable.
