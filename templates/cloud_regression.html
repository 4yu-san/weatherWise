    Methodology: how the two regression models were built and compared

    Multiple Linear Regression (the linear baseline)

    Fits one straight-line (additive) relationship: each independent variable has a fixed coefficient, and the prediction is the intercept plus each coefficient times its input. It is fast and easy to interpret. It cannot capture curves or interactions unless those are added by hand, which makes it the natural baseline.

    Random Forest Regressor (the non-linear alternative)

    Builds many decision trees on bootstrap samples of the training data and averages their outputs. The trees split on thresholds, so the forest can model non-linear effects and interactions between variables automatically. The cost is lower interpretability and a higher risk of fitting noise.

    Fair comparison & selection

    • Both models use the same 80:20 train/test split (random seed {{ metrics.random_state if metrics else 42 }}).
    • Pre-processing (standardisation for the linear model) sits inside a scikit-learn Pipeline, so it is fitted on training data only.
    • Random Forest hyper-parameters and the choice between the two models are decided with 5-fold cross-validation on the training data. {% if metrics %}{{ metrics.regression.selection_rule }}{% endif %}
    • The test set is used once, at the end, to report R², MAE and RMSE.
